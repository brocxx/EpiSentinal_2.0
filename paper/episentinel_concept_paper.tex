\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}
%% ============================================================================

Dengue fever is a severe and rapidly growing vector-borne public health challenge in Karnataka. Over the past several years, the state has experienced a marked, year-on-year increase in both dengue morbidity and spatial spread. The disease is endemic across urban and rural districts alike, placing immense stress on regional healthcare systems during peak transmission seasons.

The fundamental bottleneck in managing dengue is that the current government surveillance infrastructure, primarily anchored by the Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP), is structurally reactive. The system relies on passive reporting, where case records are collected from clinics, aggregated at the district level, and sent to state registries. This pipeline introduces a lag of two to three weeks. Consequently, by the time public health officers receive an official outbreak alert, the transmission wave has already peaked. Vector control measures and medical resources are deployed too late to prevent infections or reduce hospitalization rates.

Fortunately, there is a clear biological window of predictability. A dengue cycle is triggered by environmental shifts: heavy rainfall and high temperatures create breeding grounds for \textit{Aedes aegypti} mosquitoes. The full chain from environmental trigger to clinical outbreak spans approximately three to four weeks. Furthermore, once transmission begins, ongoing case counts encode a powerful statistical momentum: active infections directly predict new cases in the coming week.

Health officers in Karnataka currently lack an integrated tool that translates this data into early warnings, explanations, and official action recommendations in a single accessible interface.

\section{Proposed Solution}
\label{sec:solution}
%% ============================================================================

EpiSentinel is an end-to-end, web-based AI dashboard that predicts district-level dengue outbreaks in Karnataka \textbf{seven days in advance}. It bridges complex machine learning with the operational needs of non-technical public health administrators.

The prediction engine analyzes two types of signals simultaneously: (1) local epidemiological momentum --- how case counts have trended over the past four weeks --- and (2) environmental variables including temperature, rainfall, and humidity. Risk levels for all districts are visualized on an interactive, color-coded map (Low, High, Critical) enabling state-level directors to assess vulnerability at a glance.

The dashboard also explains \textit{why} a district is flagged. For example: \textit{``Risk is critical due to a sustained upward trend in local case counts over the past three weeks, modulated by heavy rainfall fourteen days ago.''}

To convert warnings into action, EpiSentinel includes an AI advisory assistant called \textbf{Sentinel AI}. It retrieves Karnataka's official Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for vector-borne disease control and generates a structured, district-specific action plan covering larviciding schedules, hospital bed allocations, and public awareness campaigns.

\section{Key Innovations}
\label{sec:innovations}
%% ============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*., leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt, topsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Case-trend-first forecasting:} Recent epidemiological momentum is the primary prediction signal, outweighing weather variables by $4\times$ at a seven-day horizon.
  \item \textbf{Per-district calibrated thresholds:} Each district's outbreak definition is derived from its own historical case distribution, not a generic statewide cutoff.
  \item \textbf{Three-stage data-leakage audit:} The pipeline is audited to prevent models from memorizing static district identifiers or historical baselines, ensuring genuine generalization.
  \item \textbf{Principled confidence intervals:} Every prediction includes a 90\% conformal confidence range (e.g., ``84\% risk, range 71\%--93\%''), quantifying model uncertainty for decision-makers.
  \item \textbf{Strictly SOP-grounded AI advice:} Sentinel AI retrieves only official Karnataka health protocols, preventing hallucinated or unverified recommendations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Technical Approach}
\label{sec:technical}
%% ============================================================================

The forecasting engine is a \textbf{Weighted Ensemble} of five complementary algorithms: XGBoost (49.5\%), CatBoost (29.7\%), Poisson Regression (10.8\%), Random Forest (9.1\%), and LightGBM (0.9\%). Sub-models were trained on 2017--2021 weekly district-level panel data and evaluated on a fully held-out 2023 test set.

Explainability is delivered via SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations), which computes each feature's contribution to a specific prediction and surfaces a plain-language driver statement per district on the map. The Sentinel AI advisory chatbot uses Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): it retrieves specific sections from the SOP vector database before generating advice, making it incapable of producing unverified guidance. The full system runs on a FastAPI backend with a D3.js interactive map frontend.

\section{Relevance and Impact}
\label{sec:relevance}
%% ============================================================================

Karnataka is home to approximately 67 million people, and dengue is endemic across most of its 31 districts. A one-week advance warning gives health authorities sufficient time to deploy fogging teams, pre-position IV fluids and platelet concentrates, and alert primary health centers --- actions that directly reduce morbidity. This aligns with India's National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP) mandate for proactive community-level interventions.

It is worth noting that ARTPARK at the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru, has built an operational system called PRISM-H for Karnataka. EpiSentinel is an independent student research project exploring complementary innovations: local SHAP explainability, principled conformal uncertainty quantification, and SOP-grounded RAG advisories not present in existing operational frameworks. The full research paper is being prepared for IEEE journal submission.

EpiSentinel shifts Karnataka's dengue response from reactive to proactive by combining robust ensemble forecasting, local explainability, principled uncertainty quantification, and SOP-grounded advisory generation in a single deployable platform.
